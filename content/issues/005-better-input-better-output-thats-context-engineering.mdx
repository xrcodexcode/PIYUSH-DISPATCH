# Issue #3: Better Input, Better Output. That's Context Engineering.

*The AI isn't broken. It just doesn't know what you know.*

![Context Engineering — Organizing the right information before you ask](/assets/issue-3/1.jpg)

I see the same complaint everywhere.

![The frustration of getting generic AI output](/assets/issue-3/2.jpg)

"I tried ChatGPT and the output was generic." "The AI doesn't understand what I want." "I gave it a clear prompt and it still got it wrong."

I've been there too. And after spending way too long tweaking the wording of my prompts, I realized something that changed how I use every AI tool:

**The problem usually isn't *how* you ask. It's *what you give it to work with.***

That idea has a name. It's called **context engineering**. And once you understand it, you'll never look at a bad AI output the same way again.

---

## So what is context engineering?

Here is the simplest way I can put it:

> A **prompt** is what you *ask*.
> **Context** is everything you *give it to work with*.

Context engineering is the skill of providing an AI with the right background, the right constraints, and the right examples — so it can actually produce something useful.

Most people blame the model when they get a bad result. In my experience, the real issue is almost always missing context. The AI isn't stupid. It's just working with incomplete information.

You wouldn't expect a brilliant stranger to write your quarterly report without telling them who it's for, what data to use, or how long it should be.

But that's exactly what we do with AI all the time.

---

## Think of it like briefing a new teammate

This is the analogy that made it click for me.

![Briefing a new teammate with the right context](/assets/issue-3/3.jpg)

Imagine you hire someone talented. Really talented. It's their first day at the company. They know nothing about your team, your projects, or your preferences.

You walk over and say:

> "Write the report."

They'd freeze. *What report? For whom? What format? What's the deadline? What happened last quarter?*

Now imagine you say:

> "Write a one-page summary of last quarter's sales for the VP of Marketing. Use bullet points. Focus on the top 3 regions by revenue. Here's the raw data and here's a report from last quarter you can use as a reference."

Same person. Same brain. Completely different result.

That's context engineering in a nutshell.

You're not making the AI smarter. You're giving it what it needs to do its best work. And the difference in output quality is night and day.

---

## See the difference

![Bad Context vs Good Context — The same model, two very different results](/assets/issue-3/4.jpg)

Let me show you what this looks like in practice.

**❌ Weak prompt:**

> "Write me a LinkedIn post."

The AI has to guess your tone, your topic, your audience, the length, the goal. It guesses wrong on most of them. The result feels generic because the input *was* generic.

**✅ Prompt with context:**

> "Write a LinkedIn post for software engineers. Topic: why side projects help career growth. Tone: conversational and encouraging. Under 150 words. End with a question to drive comments."

Same model. Same capability. But the second prompt works because I told it *who* it's for, *what* to write about, *how* it should sound, *how long* it should be, and *how* to end.

That's not prompt magic. That's just giving better context.

---

## The context pipeline

I use a simple mental framework whenever I write an important prompt. Good context usually has four parts:

![The Context Pipeline — Goal, Background, Constraints, Example, Result](/assets/issue-3/5.jpg)

```text
🎯 Goal  →  📋 Background  →  🚧 Constraints  →  💡 Example  →  ✅ Better Result
```

| Piece | What it does | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Goal** | What you want | "Write a product description" |
| **Background** | What the AI needs to know | "It's a noise-canceling headphone for remote workers" |
| **Constraints** | Rules and boundaries | "Under 80 words. No technical jargon." |
| **Example** | What good looks like | "Here's a description from our site we like…" |

You don't always need all four. But the more you provide, the less the AI has to guess — and guessing is where things go wrong.

---

## Why this actually matters

![Context engineering powers real-world AI systems](/assets/issue-3/6.jpg)

Context engineering isn't just a prompting trick for getting better ChatGPT answers. It's the core skill behind every AI system that actually works in the real world.

- **Chatbots** that answer customer questions correctly — because they receive the right documents, not the entire knowledge base.
- **AI agents** that complete tasks reliably — because someone defined the rules and boundaries clearly.
- **Writing tools** that match your brand voice — because you showed them examples of what good looks like.
- **Automation** that doesn't break — because someone thought about the edge cases upfront.

Every production AI system you've ever used runs on carefully engineered context behind the scenes. The prompt you see is just the tip of the iceberg.

## The takeaway

![Master context, master the output](/assets/issue-3/7.jpg)

> **You don't need a better model. You need to give your model better context.**
>
> The best AI results come from the best inputs — not the best prompts alone.
> Master context, and you master the output.

## ❓ Try it yourself

Here's a challenge. Take a prompt you've used recently — one where the output disappointed you.

Before rewriting the words, ask yourself one question:

*Did I give the AI enough context to succeed?*

In my experience, that single question will improve your results more than any prompt trick ever will. 🧠
